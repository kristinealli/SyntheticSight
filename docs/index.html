1 Problem & Motivation

Human judgment is not enough

Synthetic faces can be hard to spot and feel trustworthy.

In Nightingale and Farid’s 2022 study, participants were 48% accurate at distinguishing real faces from synthetic ones. After training, average accuracy rose only to 59%. In a separate experiment, participants rated synthetic faces as 7.7% more trustworthy than real faces.

Detection belongs inside digital literacy: it should support human review, not replace judgment or become absolute proof.

5 Impact Analysis

Useful - with a caveat

A screening tool—not a source of truth.

Positive Impact - Our Model:

  • Can support media literacy and deepfake awareness.
  • Can help flag suspicious images for appropriate human review.
  • Provides a reproducible baseline for future testing.

Potential Risks To Be Aware Of:

  • False positives may damage trust or reputation.
  • False negatives may create a false sense of security.
  • Detector performance may degrade as generators evolve.
Read the responsible-AI analysis →

Key takeaway

Strong benchmark separation does not mean deepfake detection is solved.

People can struggle to distinguish real faces from AI-generated ones. On this benchmark, Synthetic Sight’s fine-tuned ResNet-50 distinguished real FFHQ faces from StyleGAN-generated faces with very high accuracy. However, the model is a screening tool—not definitive proof of an image’s source, history, or authenticity.
